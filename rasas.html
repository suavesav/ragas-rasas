॥ नवरस ॥

The Ras

The emotional essences of Bharata Muni's Natyashastra

Ras (रस) — literally "juice" or "essence" — is the theory of aesthetic emotion at the heart of all Indian classical arts. Formulated by the sage Bharata Muni in the Natyashastra (c. 200 BCE–200 CE), the doctrine holds that art does not express the artist's personal emotion but rather distills universal emotional essences that the audience tastes. A listener does not feel sad when hearing Karuna ras; they experience the aesthetic form of sadness — purified, impersonal, and beautiful. The original eight ras were later expanded to nine with the addition of Shanta by the Kashmiri aesthetician Abhinavagupta. This site adds a tenth, Bhakti (devotion), which the Vaishnava theorist Rupa Goswami raised to the status of a ras in the sixteenth century and which the Hindustani sources use more often than any other single mood word.
